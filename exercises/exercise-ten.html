Multiplot It!

Combining multiple individual charts into an interactive dashboard using Plotly and Quarto! This exercise expands upon the Module 7 visualisaton that I made multivariate.

Exercise Summary

For this exercise, I created a multiplot dashboard that brings together multiple data visualisations to explore relationships between different variables. I decided to build on an existing data visualisation, by Pew Research Center, by including additional datasets and presenting this information in an interactive dashboard. I chose to investigate the Political Unhappiness across 25 countries, and more specifically, how this could potentially be related to GDP per capita, perceived corruption, and income inequality.

My Process

I began with a visualisation from Pew Research Center than I had previously used in Module 7, and sourced data from the United Nations, Transparency International and the World Bank to introduce population, corruption, and income inequality as additional variables. Using Python, I combined all of this data into a single Dataframe, where each country has its associated % of unhappiness, population, corruption index (CPI) and gini index. After creating these charts using Plotly, I combined the three into a single interactive dashboard using Quarto.

Summary

This interactive dashboard explores the relationship between political unhappiness across 25 countries and GDP per capita, corruption index and income inequality. The dashboard consists of three scatter plots, with each comparing the percentage of adults who believe their political system requires major changes or complete reform against each of the listed variables. Countries are represented by labelled bubbles, with bubble size representing population size.

The dashboard was inspired by Pew Research Center's U.S. stands out among high-income nations for unhappiness with political system, which originally compared political unhappiness with GDP per capita. The multivariate versions extend the original by using population data from the United Nations World Population Prospects 2024, corruption data from Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index 2025, and income inequality data from the World Bank's Gini index.

Mapping

Chart 1: GDP per capita and political unhappiness

  • X-axis: GDP per capita, in USD, for each of the 25 countries.
  • Y-axis: Percentage of adults in each country who say their political system needs major changes or complete reform.
  • Bubble Size: Population of each country in millions. Larger bubbles represent countries with larger populations, while smaller bubbles represent countries with smaller populations.
  • Colour: The U.S. is highlighted in blue, while the remaining countries are coloured grey, since Pew Research Center's original visualisation emphasised the U.S.

Chart 2: Perceived corruption and political unhappiness

  • X-axis: Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) score for 2025, ranging from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (very clean).
  • Y-axis: Percentage of adults in each country who say their political system needs major changes or complete reform.
  • Bubble Size: Population of each country in millions. Larger bubbles represent countries with larger populations, while smaller bubbles represent countries with smaller populations.
  • Colour: The U.S. is highlighted in blue, while the remaining countries are coloured grey, maintaining the emphasis used in the original Pew Research Center visualisation.

Chart 3: Income inequality and political dissatisfaction

  • X-axis: Gini index, using the most recent available value for each country. Higher values indicate greater income inequality.

Important Notes

Pew Research Center

  • The political attitudes data are based on Pew Research Center's Spring 2025 Global Attitudes Survey, which surveyed 31,938 adults across 25 countries.
  • The survey results represent public views rather than objective measurements of the performance of each country's political system.
  • GDP per capita is expressed in USD.
  • The GDP values used in the original data visualisation were sourced from the World Bank and accessed by Pew Research Center on March 23, 2026.
  • The trend line does not demonstrate a causal relationship between GDP per capita and unhappiness towards political systems.

United Nations

  • The UN states that the 2024 Revision contains population estimates informed by censuses, registration systems and nationally representative surveys.
  • The populations for January 1, 2026 are used.
  • The dataset used is a Standard Projection in CSV format.
  • The dataset has been cleaned to include only the 25 countries used and to convert population from thousands into millions.
  • The bubble sizes have been scaled by a factor of 1.5 million.

Transparency International

  • The Corruption Perceptions Index (2025) measures perceived levels of public-sector corruption using assessments from experts and businesspeople.
  • CPI scores range from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (very clean).
  • The CPI measures perceptions of corruption rather than directly observed corruption.
  • The CPI data have been processed and standardised, with the final values being averages to yield higher accuracy.

World Bank

  • The Gini index measures the distribution of income within a country and does not directly measure poverty or general wealth.
  • The values range from 0 (perfect equality) to 100 (perfect inequality).
  • The most recent available Gini index value is used for each country.
  • Gini indices are not calculated annually, so reference years differ between countries due to variations in data availability.
Access
  • The political system and GDP per capita data used in Pew Research Center's original visualisation can be accessed through the original visualisation page and downloaded as a CSV file.
  • The full survey data can also be accessed through Pew Research Center's Spring 2025 Survey Data dataset, although a free account is required for download.
  • The United Nations World Population Prospects 2024 dataset is available in several formats, including CSV.
  • Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index 2025 scores and related documentation can be downloaded as a ZIP archive containing CSV and PDF files.
  • The World Bank's Gini index data can be accessed through World Bank Open Data and downloaded in several formats, including CSV.
