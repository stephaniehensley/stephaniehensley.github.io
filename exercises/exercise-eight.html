Exercise 08

Module 8 Exercise

Creating a spatial data visualisation on an interesting topic and source of my choice!

Exercise Summary

For this exercise, I decided to investigate the relationship between recorded meteorites and their geographic location using spatial data from NASA Open Data Portal’s Meteorite Landing dataset.

I chose to create a proportional symbol/bubble map to display the global distribution of recorded meteorites. Each meteorite is positioned according to its latitude and longitude, is coloured by the time period it was recorded, and is sized according to its mass using a logarithmic scale.

Recorded Meteorites Around the World

Visualisation Approach

  • Each meteorite is positioned according to its recorded latitude and longitude.
  • Colour represents the recorded time period, grouped into six ordinal categories ranging from Before 1800 to 2000 Onwards.
  • Marker size represents the recorded mass of each meteorite using a logarithmic scale.
  • Larger circles therefore represent meteorites with greater recorded masses, while smaller circles represent meteorites with smaller recorded masses.
  • Records with missing latitude, longitude, year or mass values were excluded from the visualisation.
  • Records located at 0° latitude and 0° longitude were also excluded because a large number of records contained these coordinates.

Data Card

The following data card summarises the dataset, visual mappings and important considerations for interpreting the spatial visualisation.

Title Recorded Meteorites Around the World

Colour shows the recorded time period, while larger circles represent meteorites with greater mass.
Summary

The spatial visualisation shows the global distribution of recorded meteorites around the world using data from NASA's ‘Meteorite Landings’ dataset, sourced from The Meteoritical Society.

Each marker represents an individual meteorite record and is located at its corresponding latitude and longitude. The colour represents the time period in which the meteorite was recorded, ranging from Before 1800 to 2000 Onwards, and the marker size represents its mass.

Data Source

National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). (2025, May 30). Meteorite Landings [Data set]. NASA Open Data Portal. Accessed September 30, 2026. https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/meteorite-landings

Mapping
  • Geographic Position: Each marker is positioned using the recorded latitude and longitude of the meteorite.
  • Colour: The recorded time period of each meteorite. These are grouped into six ordinal categories: Before 1800, 1800–1849, 1850–1899, 1900–1949, 1950–1999, and 2000 Onwards.
  • Marker Size: The recorded mass of each meteorite using a logarithmic scale; larger circles indicate larger masses, and smaller circles indicate smaller masses.
Important Notes
  • The map includes recorded meteorites within the given dataset and does not show every meteorite that has ever landed on Earth.
  • Meteorites with missing latitude, longitude, year or mass were excluded.
  • Records located at (0°, 0°) were excluded, as there was a very large number of them within the dataset.
  • Meteorite mass is measured in grams in the original dataset, but the legend displays some masses in kilograms to make differences in marker size easier to interpret.
  • The marker size uses a logarithmic scale and is not directly proportional to the raw mass.
  • Recorded years have been grouped into broader time periods.
  • Clustering of meteorite records does not mean that they are more likely to fall in the recorded location. This is only indicative of more observations occurring in the clustering regions.
Access

You can access a copy of the data used to construct this spatial visualisation through NASA's Open Data Portal ‘Meteorite Landings’ dataset.
