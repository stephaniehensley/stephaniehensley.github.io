Exercise Summary

For this exercise, I created an interactive data visualisation that demonstrates intentional interactive design. I chose to build upon my previous meteorite visualisation from Module 8 using NASA’s Meteorite Landings dataset, which allows the users to explore individual meteorites, via hover information, and over time, via a time series implementation. Since I was already colouring the meteorites as per their time period of recording, it made the most sense to add some time-based interactivity, hence, I’ve made it so the users can visually explore the accumulation of recorded meteorites over time, while also checking the individual characteristics of a particular meteorite.

Title Recorded Meteorites Around the World

Colour shows the recorded time period, while larger circles represent meteorites with greater mass.

Summary

This interactive spatial visualisation shows the global distribution of recorded meteorites around the world using data from NASA's 'Meteorite Landings' dataset, sourced from The Meteoritical Society. Each marker represents an individual meteorite record and is located at its corresponding latitude and longitude. The colour represents the time period in which the meteorite was recorded, ranging from Before 1800 to 2000 Onwards and the marker size represents its mass.

Users can use the interactive time slider and animation controls to explore how recorded meteorites have accumulated over time, and can also hover over individual meteorites to see their associated characteristics.

Data Source

National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). (2025, May 30). Meteorite Landings [Data set]. NASA Open Data Portal. Accessed September 30, 2026. https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/meteorite-landings

Mapping
  • Geographic Position: Each marker is positioned using the recorded latitude and longitude of the meteorite.
  • Colour: The recorded time period of each meteorite. These are grouped into 6 ordinal categories: Before 1800, 1800-1849, 1850-1899, 1900-1949, 1950-1999, and 2000 Onwards.
  • Marker Size: The recorded mass of each meteorite using a logarithmic scale; larger circles indicate larger masses, and smaller circles indicate smaller masses.
Important Notes
  • The map includes recorded meteorites within the given dataset and does not show every meteorite that has ever landed on Earth.
  • Meteorites with missing latitude, longitude, year or mass were excluded.
  • Records located at (0°, 0°) were excluded, as there was a very large number of them within the dataset.
  • Meteorite mass is measured in grams in the original dataset, but the legend displays some masses in kilograms to make differences in marker size easier to interpret.
  • The marker size uses a logarithmic scale and is not directly proportional to the raw mass.
  • Recorded years have been grouped into broader time periods.
  • Clustering of meteorite records does not mean that they are more likely to fall in the recorded location. This is only indicative of more observations occurring in the clustering regions.
Access

You can access a copy of the data used to construct this spatial visualisation through NASA's Open Data Portal 'Meteorite Landings' dataset.
